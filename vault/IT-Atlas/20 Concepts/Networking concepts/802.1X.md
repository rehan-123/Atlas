# 802.1X

> [!abstract] In one line
> Port-based network access control: devices authenticate before getting network access.

> [!tip] Think of it like
> 802.1X is a bouncer at every network port: devices must prove who they are before getting on the network.

> [!info]+ Words on this page
> **certificate**: A digital ID card that proves a website or device is who it claims to be and enables encryption.
> **port**: A numbered door on a device that a specific service listens on, for example 443 for HTTPS or 3389 for RDP.
> *More in the [[Glossary]].*

## How it works

- Supplicant (client), authenticator (switch or AP) and authentication server (RADIUS, such as NPS).
- EAP-TLS with certificates is the strongest method; PEAP-MSCHAPv2 uses passwords.
