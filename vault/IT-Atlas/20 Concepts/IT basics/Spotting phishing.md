# Spotting phishing

> [!abstract] In one line
> Fake emails, texts or calls that trick people into clicking, paying or giving passwords.

> [!tip] Think of it like
> A con artist in a delivery uniform: the uniform looks right, but the story and the rush give him away.

## How it works

- Warning signs: urgency, unexpected attachments, a sender address that doesn't match the name, requests to change bank details, sign-in pages reached from an email link.
- Hover over links to see the real address before clicking.
- Confirm odd requests by phone on a number you already know.
- Report it with the Report button; reporting quickly protects everyone.

## Check yourself

Answer in your head first, then click to reveal.

> [!question]- Name three signs an email may be phishing.
> Urgency or threats, a sender address that doesn't match, unexpected links or attachments, or requests for passwords, payment or bank changes.
