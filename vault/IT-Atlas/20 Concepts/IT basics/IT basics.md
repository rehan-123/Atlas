# IT basics

How computers, networks, accounts and the cloud work, explained from zero.

Part of [[Concepts]].

- [[How a computer works]]: A computer has a brain (CPU), short-term memory (RAM) and long-term storage (disk).
- [[Operating system]]: The main software that runs the computer and lets apps use its hardware.
- [[Files, folders and drives]]: Where things are saved: on this PC, on a network share, or in the cloud.
- [[Local vs cloud files]]: Local files live only on this device; cloud files live online and can sync to many devices.
- [[Drivers]]: Small programs that teach the operating system how to talk to a piece of hardware.
- [[User accounts and permissions]]: Who you are (account) and what you're allowed to open or change (permissions).
- [[How the internet reaches your desk]]: From the internet provider, through the firewall and switches, to your Wi-Fi or cable.
- [[IP addresses]]: The number every device needs to talk on a network, like a phone number.
- [[Wi-Fi basics]]: Wireless networking: radio signals between your device and an access point.
- [[Why restarting works]]: A restart clears memory, stops stuck programs and finishes updates.
- [[Updates and why they matter]]: Updates fix security holes and bugs; skipping them leaves known doors open.
- [[Cache and cookies]]: Saved bits of websites and apps that make them faster but can get stale or broken.
- [[Domain-joined vs cloud-joined PCs]]: How a business PC is tied to the company: to on-premises Active Directory, to Entra ID in the cloud, or both.
- [[Passwords and password managers]]: Long unique passwords, stored in a password manager, plus MFA.
- [[Spotting phishing]]: Fake emails, texts or calls that trick people into clicking, paying or giving passwords.
- [[Backups for everyone]]: Copies of data kept somewhere else so it can be restored after loss, theft or ransomware.
