# Text message or phone call scam

> [!abstract] What's going on
> A user got a text or call pretending to be IT, a bank or a boss, asking for codes, passwords or payments.

> [!tip] Think of it like
> A stranger on the phone claiming to be your bank: hang up and call the number on your card.

**Who can fix it:** Anyone can do the first steps; a tech does the rest. · **Area:** [[Security & scam problems]]

> [!info]+ Words on this page
> **MFA**: Multi-factor authentication: password plus a second proof, such as a phone prompt.
> **escalate**: Hand a problem to a more senior person or team, with your notes.
> *More in the [[Glossary]].*

## Try this first

Anyone can do these, and they fix most cases.

1. Don't share codes, passwords or approve MFA prompts for a caller.
2. Hang up and call back on a number you already know.
3. Report it to IT.

## If you're the tech

Words in CAPITALS are placeholders: replace them with your own values.

- If a code or password was shared: reset the password, revoke sessions, check MFA methods.
- Warn colleagues if it's targeting your company.

## Common causes and fixes

| Cause | Fix |
|---|---|
| Shared an MFA code | Reset and revoke sessions. |
| Gift card request from 'the boss' | Verify in person or by known number. |

## Escalate when

- Money was sent or credentials were shared

## Write it up

Copy this into the ticket and fill it in:

```
Device:

```

## Check yourself

Answer in your head first, then click to reveal.

> [!question]- A caller from 'IT' asks for the code just sent to your phone. What do you do?
> Refuse and hang up. Real IT never needs your MFA code.
